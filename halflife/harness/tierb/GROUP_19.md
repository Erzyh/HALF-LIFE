## Observations

- Role mix is inverted relative to group 1: only 19 of 49 rows are role S and 3 are role B (a live model directly evaluated or used as a baseline), while 16 are role C (mention/citation only - background sentences, related-work citations, or numbers copied from another paper's table) and 11 are role A (judge, annotator, or synthetic-data generator). Role C/A together (27 of 49) account for most of the ineligible verdicts, independent of prompt or data quality, per the schema's requirement that eligible/maybe be role S or B.
- Alias resolution is dominated by the bare-alias date rule: 40 of 49 rows carry an undated `gpt-4-turbo` mention in a paper published well after 2024-07-01 with no 1106/0125 preview string nearby, so all resolve mechanically to `gpt-4-turbo-2024-04-09`; none of this group's papers pin the exact snapshot in text the way several group-1 papers did. Three papers instead have an undated `GPT-4o` accessed after the 2024-10-02 cutoff (W7113907881, W4409984762 - both also blocked by `webui` since the model was reached through the ChatGPT/consumer site, not an API; W7204462071 has no live id resolvable at all). One paper (W4403574122) evaluates `GPT-4-0125`, a dead preview, in its results tables while using bare `gpt-4-turbo` only for synthetic data generation. Two papers use the newer `gpt-4.1-nano` id directly (W7165816860, W7161167168).
- "No usable number in text" is the single largest ineligibility reason (33 of 38 ineligible rows carry `no_number`), and it comes in several distinct flavors that are easy to conflate: (a) pure citation/review papers that never call an LLM themselves (W7204462071, W7169835348, W4417143091, W7166900952, W4415201012, W4404089139, W7127541416 - systematic reviews, meta-analyses, or "comparison to prior work" tables that copy other papers' numbers); (b) LLM-as-judge papers where the live model only scores the authors' own new method's outputs (W7167154027, W7166884525, W7163598027, W7172067881 - all RLHF/hallucination-mitigation papers using GPT-4/GPT-4-Turbo as an AlpacaEval- or vision-judge); (c) synthetic-data-generator uses where the live model built a training or augmentation corpus but is never itself benchmarked (W7166837174, W7164040240, W4416036705, W4403574122's question generator); and (d) papers where a real capability number exists but only inside a figure/chart, never as printed text (W7165816860's classification sweep, W4404390850's persuasion-completion figures, W4406231190's parsing comparison).
- Ensemble/entanglement problems produced two otherwise-strong ineligible cases: W4401043064's shared-task system never reports GPT-4 in isolation, only blended into majority-vote ensembles, and W7202365389 turned out to be a pure statistical re-analysis of already-published agent-benchmark result files (τ2-bench, AppWorld, TheAgentCompany) rather than a paper that calls any model itself, despite naming GPT-4.1, o4-mini and GPT-4-Turbo prominently.
- `data_closed` (5 rows) mostly pairs with role A/C papers that also fail on `no_number`, so it rarely changes a verdict by itself. One `dead_alias` case (W4403574122) and two `webui`+`alias_unpinned` medical papers (W7113907881, W4409984762, both using the ChatGPT consumer site for a single or 81-question demonstration) round out the blocker set.
- The 5 eligible papers all pin the live id explicitly in text (a footnote/date string or a model-spec table), print the actual prompt template, and point to a public dataset with a clear item count: W4403580145 (SEEDBench, n=14,232), W4403574994 (SP-10Axes, n=133), W4416034248 (Multi-Plan, n=204, released on HuggingFace), W7106207151 (TRIAGE-adult, n=54, code+data on GitHub), and W7172029025 (200 public health-education videos, prompt in a named supplementary file). All five are low-cost (at most a few hundred calls) except W4403580145, whose SEEDBench cell is high-tier from item count alone (a same-table MMVet cell at n=218 would be far cheaper).
- The 6 maybe papers share the "prompt partial, clear external template" gap rather than an unclear n: three reuse another benchmark's own published prompt/format without reprinting it (W4401201931 borrows TravelPlanner's zero-shot prompt; W7151420297 and W7161167168 build on MMLU/MetaMedQA's standard question format); two describe a simple, fully-specified but unquoted template (W4407687699's four prompt variants, W4402466846's report-plus-question context); and W4403346617 is missing only the ChEBI-20 test-set size, not the prompt. W7161167168 is also unusual in that its `text_path` resolved to a Zenodo landing page (title + abstract only), not the full PDF, so its methods/prompt appendix could not be inspected directly.
