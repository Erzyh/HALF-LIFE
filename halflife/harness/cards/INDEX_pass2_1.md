## What the repository audit changed

1. **Repository access decided two of the four cards, in opposite directions.** MedQA-CS releases every subject output and every judge verdict, and that completeness is exactly what killed the card: the six output keys are `gpt-4o`, `gpt-4-1106-preview`, `gpt-3.5-turbo-1106` and three Claude-3 snapshots, with a single examiner key `gpt-4-1106-preview`. Re-scoring the released InfoGatherQA verdicts gives 62.74 against the printed 62.79, which pins Table 3's "GPT-4" row to the dead 1106 preview — the paper's prose "GPT-4-turbo" is the marketing name of that preview, not the `gpt-4-turbo` alias. Justice or Prejudice went the other way: its `promptTemplate.py` is the source string behind Figure 13, and it confirms the no-tie verdict vocabulary and that the padded answers are frozen in the release.
2. **"gpt-4-turbo" remains a weak census signal.** Of the three papers the census matched on that alias, only one (W4403885225) actually used `gpt-4-turbo-0409`, and it is the one that prints the snapshot in a table. One is an alias false positive for a dead preview (W4403851744) and one names no snapshot at all (W4411120327). Papers that print a dated snapshot — W4416074906 in its Appendix G, W4403885225 in its Table 8 — are the only ones where the live arm needed no inference.
3. **Judges and evaluators die too, and they are part of the metric.** Three of the four claims are judge-mediated. MedQA-CS's metric *is* gpt-4-1106-preview; the legal paper derives even its accuracy column from a GPT-4o conclusion classifier whose undated alias resolves to the retired `gpt-4o-2024-08-06`; only AgentMisalignment scores its target task with rule-based code. Where the judge is dead, the subject arm being alive does not make the claim re-executable, and a judge substitution has to be calibrated against the released original outputs before any new subject call is bought.
4. **The cheapest card is the agentic one.** AgentMisalignment's Household Management task is 36 samples × 2 turns with no Docker, no tools and no grader model, so three seeds cost about a dollar — an order of magnitude less than the two judge-call cards. Its risk is not cost but resolution: Table 5 cells are multiples of 1/24 over six scenario variants, and the CMS can only read reasoning the model chooses to emit (the paper already reports a flat 0.000 for o4-mini on Canary Code for exactly that reason).
5. **Data, not code, still gates the legal paper.** Its repository now supplies the exact prompt — including a `Task:` system line the paper never prints — and shows the accuracy column is LLM-extracted rather than regex-parsed, but the 175 Civ. Pro. items come from a SemEval-restricted, textbook-derived corpus released only against a signed form with a delete-after-use clause.
